## What a small shop actually needs from a POS

Strip away the sales talk and a small shop needs a POS to do a small number of jobs well.

- **Sell fast.** A customer should not wait while you hunt for a price. Scanning a barcode ends that problem.
- **Take money the way customers pay.** Cash, M-Pesa, and sometimes both in one sale. Not one or the other — both.
- **Know what you have.** Roughly. You do not need lab-level precision, but you should stop guessing whether you have four crates of soda or one.
- **Know who did what.** One till, sometimes two or three people behind it. You want to know who sold what, who voided a sale, and who opened the drawer.
- **Keep selling when the internet drops.** This is Kenya. KPLC and Safaricom have good days and bad days.
- **Be affordable.** A shop doing modest daily turnover cannot carry heavy monthly software fees.

That is the whole list. Everything else is a bonus you may grow into later.

## What you do NOT need yet (avoid the upsell)

Small shops get sold software built for supermarkets. That is where money leaks.

You probably do not need:

- **Multi-currency pricing.** You sell in shillings. Full stop.
- **Complex weighted-barcode scales.** Unless you genuinely sell loose rice and sugar by weight from a scale at scale.
- **Elaborate promotions and coupon engines.** A handwritten "offer" card on the counter does the same job.
- **Enterprise supply-chain modules.** Purchase orders are useful; full procurement workflows are not, not at one shop.
- **On-premise servers.** Modern POS runs on a phone, tablet, or cheap desktop you already own.

Every extra feature is another thing to learn and often another line on the bill. Buy what your shop does today, and leave room to add later. If someone is pushing you toward a tier built for a supermarket chain, it is worth pausing before you sign up for something you will under-use.

## How M-Pesa at the counter works

M-Pesa is not a "nice to have" in a Kenyan shop. It is frequently most of the day's takings. So the question is not whether your POS accepts M-Pesa — it is how cleanly it does.

There are two broad approaches.

**Manual M-Pesa.** The customer pays to your till number, you glance at the confirmation SMS, then key the sale in the POS as "M-Pesa" payment. It works, but it is slow, and it depends on someone reading messages correctly.

**Integrated STK push.** The POS sends a payment request straight to the customer's phone. They enter their PIN, the money moves, and the POS marks the sale as paid — automatically. No reading SMS, no manually typing amounts, no argument over whether the money landed.

For a small shop, the second approach is worth chasing: it cuts counter time and makes the close of day simpler, because the POS already knows what came in by M-Pesa versus cash.

You can also take card payments if that matters to your customers, and with the right integration, M-Pesa payments can be matched to sales automatically instead of reconciled by hand. ## Keeping the till honest: PINs, shifts and void controls

Most small-shop shrinkage is not dramatic theft. It is small, quiet, and repetitive. A sale not keyed. A void after the customer walks away. A drawer opened with no explanation.

You cannot stand at the till all day. So the software has to do some of the watching.

- **Cashier PINs and permissions.** Every person has a login. You can restrict who can void a sale, apply a discount, or open the drawer.
- **Shifts.** Open a shift at the start, close it at the end. You get the cash expected versus cash counted, per person.
- **Void and refund controls.** Voids get logged with the cashier's name attached. If voids pile up on one login, you will see it.
- **Role separation.** The owner sees everything. A part-time cashier sees only the till.

None of this is about distrust. It is about removing ambiguity. When opening the till is recorded and closing a shift produces a clean number, the awkward conversations mostly disappear — there is a record instead of a guess.

## Stock tracking without spreadsheets

Most small shops start on a notebook or a spreadsheet. Both fail the same way: they are only as current as the last person who remembered to update them, which is usually nobody.

A POS changes that because it counts as a by-product of selling. Every scanned item subtracts from stock automatically — the sale is the stock update.

For a small shop, that unlocks a few specific things:

- **Low-stock alerts.** You get told before a fast-mover runs dry, which is exactly when you still have time to order.
- **Stock-takes.** Do a physical count, enter it, and the system shows the gaps. Regular stock-takes catch both shortages and honest recording errors.
- **Supplier and purchase orders.** When goods come in, they go into stock properly rather than being forgotten.
- **One number.** Even with one shop today, keeping one source of truth means growth later is far less painful.

## When to graduate to multi-branch

One day, if things go well, you will open a second shop. It is worth knowing the signs so you do not outgrow your software.

Look for these signals:

- You cannot be at both counters, and you need one view of both.
- Stock moves between shops and you are losing track of it.
- You need proper roles — a manager at one branch, a cashier at another.
- You want a single dashboard instead of two sets of books.

When that happens, you move from a small-shop setup into a [multi-branch POS system](/blog/multi-branch-pos-system-kenya): one stock count across locations, inter-branch transfers, shifts and permissions, and one dashboard across the shops. You may also want an online storefront that shares the same prices and stock as your physical shop, so the two never disagree.

The important thing is you do not have to plan for all of that on day one. Start simple, and grow the software with the business.

## FAQ

**Do I need expensive hardware to run a POS in my duka?**
No. A barcode scanner, a thermal receipt printer, and a phone or desktop you already own are enough. The same hardware keeps working as you move up plans.

**Can I use a POS if my shop's internet is unreliable?**
Yes. Sales queue on the device while offline and sync automatically when you reconnect. You keep selling at the counter regardless of the network.

**Is a free POS actually usable for a real shop?**
Kiosk's free plan supports 300 products and one cashier, which is a real till for a small duka. You move up only when a limit — more products, more cashiers — genuinely gets in your way.

**How do I stop cashiers from voiding sales or pocketing cash?**
Use cashier PINs with permissions, run shifts that produce expected-versus-counted cash per person, and let every void be logged with a name. You cannot watch the till all day; the record does it for you.

**Can I take cash and M-Pesa in the same sale?**
Yes. Cash, M-Pesa and split payments all sit in one flow, so a customer can pay partly in shillings and partly by M-Pesa without you doing arithmetic on the side.

**Do I have to switch to a whole new system when I open a second shop?**
No. You grow into the multi-branch features — one stock count, transfers, shifts and one dashboard — rather than starting over.
